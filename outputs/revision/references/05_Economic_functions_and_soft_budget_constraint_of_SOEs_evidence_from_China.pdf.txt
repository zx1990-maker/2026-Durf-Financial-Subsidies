ABSTRACT
Existing literature has extensively explored the evolution of soft budget constraints in State-Owned 
Enterprises (SOEs) from strategic and socio-functional perspectives. However, there is a notable 
dearth of studies investigating these constraints from an economic-functional standpoint. Our 
study bridges this gap by analysing the impact of the increasing profit remittance ratio by SOEs to 
the government, using the Difference-in-Differences (DID) method. We reveal several critical 
insights: (1) As the economic function of SOEs progressively strengthens, there is a significant 
increase in government subsidies to these enterprises. Specifically, a 10% increase in the profit 
contribution ratio leads to an 18.96% increase in government subsidies. (2) Mechanistic analysis 
suggests that compensatory benefits from the government are likely the primary driver of the 
increased subsidies received by SOEs. Empirical data indicate that mandatory profit remittances 
adversely affect the profitability and growth of SOEs, with more severe effects resulting in greater 
government subsidies. (3) The reciprocal exchange of interests between governments and SOEs 
may exacerbate the principal-agent dilemmas inherent in such relationships.

I. Introduction
The concept of soft budget constraints, initially 
introduced by Kornai in 1980, characterizes 
a common phenomenon in transition economies 
where governments support state-owned enterprises 
(SOEs) through methods like subsidies. This 
approach distorts pricing dynamics in the credit 
market, channels excessive capital into inefficient 
SOEs, and amplifies the hidden debt risks faced by 
local governments (Allen et al. 2023; Dong, Hou, 
and Ni 2021; Jin, Wang, and Zhang 2023; Maskin 
and Xu 2001; Wang, Fang, and Luo 2022). Tackling 
the issue of soft budget constraints on SOEs and 
transitioning them towards market-oriented opera­
tions is critically important, attracting widespread 
focus across both theoretical and practical domains.
Numerous studies have explored the origins of 
soft budget constraints, examining them from 
diverse perspectives such as government patern­
alism (Kornai 1980, 1986), time inconsistency 
dilemmas (Dewatripont and Maskin 1995), fiscal 
federalism (Qian and Roland 1998), the property 
characteristics of SOEs (Li 1992), and the societal 
roles fulfilled by SOEs (Lin, Cai, and Li 1998; Lin 
and Li 2008; Lin and Tan 1999). However, 
a significant gap remains in the literature regard­
ing how the economic functions of SOEs – parti­
cularly their remittance of funds to the 
government – impact soft budget constraints.
This paper exploit the State Capital Operation 
Program (hereafter the programme) in China, to 
determine whether the economic functions of 
SOEs exacerbate soft budget constraints. The pro­
gramme intends to form a fiscal fund by requiring 
state-owned enterprises groups, which are 100% 
owned by the central government, to contribute 
a portion of their consolidated after-tax income in 
the form of mandated dividends. Utilizing data 
from 2004 to 2014 and employing a Difference-in- 
Differences (DID) model, the findings reveal that 
SOEs affected by the programme received signifi­
cantly higher subsidies compared to unaffected 
entities. Specifically, for every 10% increase in the 
profit contribution ratio, there was an average 
increase of 18.96% in government subsidies allo­
cated to these enterprises.
