# Midterm Report
> **Project objective:** This project estimates the asset-side return gap between CSI300 state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and private enterprises using a counterfactual prediction framework.
>
> **Core idea:** Financial characteristics of private enterprises are used to train Ridge prediction models. The trained models are then applied to SOEs to estimate their counterfactual EBI/A, ROA, and ROE under the hypothetical condition that they operated as private firms. The main outcome is defined as:
>
> `Gap = predicted value − actual value`

---

## 1. Summary of Major Revisions

| # | Revision | Type | Main Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Three benchmark frameworks | Research design | The pooled model is heavily driven by `market_A`; the CSI-only benchmark is more institutionally comparable. |
| 2 | 2018–2025 panel data | Data structure | The training sample increased by 6.4 times and covers seven prediction periods. |
| 3 | Multiple targets: EBI/A + ROA + ROE | Robustness | The three targets show consistent directions, while ROE has the weakest fit. |
| 4 | A/H market-environment control | Model specification | The gap increases by 1.4 percentage points, or about 60%, but the single-dummy assumption is too strong. |
| 5 | Financial-sector filtering and stricter non-SOE definition | Sample definition | Only private enterprises are retained; public, foreign-owned, and collectively owned firms are excluded. |
| 6 | Common-support screening | Inference boundary | The support rate is 71.9%; the common-support gap is +1.67 percentage points. |
| 7 | PSM + CEM + EntropyBal + OverlapWgt | Causal identification | SOEs and private enterprises barely overlap; all weighting and matching methods fail to achieve sufficient balance. |
| 8 | Placebo + transportability + rolling-window checks | Model diagnosis | The model shows no systematic prediction bias, but cross-market prediction fails seriously. |

## 2. Detailed Description of Each Revision

---

### 2.1 Initial Baseline Model

**Specification:** 2024 features are used to predict 2025 EBI/A. This is a single-period cross-sectional model trained on H-share non-SOEs and used to predict CSI300 SOEs.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | Ridge(α=10) |
| Features | FirmSize, MarketShare, HHI, AssetTurnover, WorkingCapitalRatio, FixedAssetsRatio, FinancialLeverage, CurrentRatio, CapexRatio; all measured in 2024 |
| Categorical variables | First-level and second-level industry dummies |
| Winsorization | Clip at 5%–95%; observations are not deleted |
| Training sample | 186 H-share non-SOEs |
| Prediction sample | 118 CSI300 SOEs |

**Baseline result:**

| Benchmark | N Train | Mean Gap | Asset-Weighted Gap | Positive Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| H_nonSOE | 186 | +2.59pp | +3.06pp | 74.6% |

**Limitations:**

- Only one benchmark is used.
- The model relies on single-period data, so `N=186` is sensitive to one-year shocks.
- The only target variable is EBI/A.
- No market-environment control is included, so H-share and A-share differences are not separated.
- The non-SOE definition is too broad and includes public, foreign-owned, and collectively owned enterprises.
- No sample-comparability screening is conducted; all non-SOEs are used for training.

---

### 2.2 Revision 1: Three-Benchmark Framework

**Motivation:** A single H-share benchmark cannot distinguish market-environment differences from ownership-efficiency differences.

**Implementation:** A unified modeling interface is used:

`X_{i,t-1} → Y_{i,t}`

The model is trained and evaluated under three separate benchmarks: `H_nonSOE`, `CSI_nonSOE`, and `Pooled`.

**Results:**

| Benchmark | N Train (Panel) | EBI/A Mean Gap | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| H_nonSOE | 1,139 | +0.75pp | H-share benchmark |
| CSI_nonSOE | 685 | −0.41pp | A-share same-market benchmark |
| **Pooled** | **1,824** | **+2.33pp** | Includes `market_A` |

**Limitation:** The pooled estimate of +2.33pp was later shown to be largely generated by the single `market_A` dummy. Therefore, it should not be treated as the main result.

---

### 2.3 Revision 2: 2018–2025 Panel Data

**Motivation:** A single-period dataset with `N=284` is strongly affected by one-year macroeconomic fluctuations. Panel data greatly increases sample size and covers different macro environments.

**Implementation:**

1. **Wide-to-long transformation:** Each firm-year is split into two rows:
   - Feature-year row, such as `year=2023`: features are observed, while target is missing.
   - Target-year row, such as `year=2024`: target is observed, while contemporaneous features are missing.

2. **Lagged-feature construction:**  
   `groupby(firm_id).shift(1)` is used to create variables such as `FirmSize_lag`, which equals the previous year’s FirmSize.

3. **Seven prediction periods are stacked:**  
   2019, 2020, ..., 2025.

**Results:**

|  | Single Period | Panel | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training sample, pooled | 284 | 1,824 | **×6.4** |
| CV R², winsorized | — | 0.234 | Evaluatable |

**Limitations:**

- Year fixed effects have not yet been implemented in the main pipeline, although the feature-engineering module has already been written.
- Industry-by-year interaction fixed effects have not yet been implemented.
- A formal rolling-window out-of-time CV has not yet been fully incorporated; it has only been run as a diagnostic check.

---

### 2.4 Revision 3: Multi-Target Prediction: EBI/A + ROA + ROE

**Motivation:** The main target, EBI/A, requires robustness checks. ROA is used to examine asset profitability from another accounting perspective, while ROE is used to observe shareholder-return differences.

**Implementation:** ROA and ROE data from `data_input/ROA/` and `data_input/ROE/` are loaded from 2015–2025 wide-format files, merged into the panel by `(firm_id, year)`, and retained only on target-year rows.

**Results:**

| Target | Pooled Mean Gap | CV R², Winsorized | CV RMSE | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| ★ EBI/A | **+2.33pp** | 0.234 | 0.100 | Main target |
| ROA | **+2.54pp** | 0.242 | 0.121 | Same direction |
| ROE | **+2.75pp** | 0.147 | 0.179 | Supplementary target; lowest R² |

**Limitations:**

- ROE has an R² of only about 0.15, much lower than EBI/A and ROA. This confirms that ROE is not suitable as the main target.
- Duplicate rows were generated during the first ROA/ROE merge and have already been fixed.

---

### 2.5 Revision 4: A/H Market-Environment Control

**Motivation:** The pooled benchmark combines H-share and A-share private enterprises. Without controlling for market environment, the model confounds market differences with ownership differences.

**Implementation:**

```text
market_A = 1 for A-share / CSI300 firms
market_A = 0 for H-share firms

Pooled model: Y = f(X_{t-1}, market_A)

When predicting SOEs: market_A = 1, meaning A-share market environment
```

`market_A` is added to the feature matrix as a binary numeric variable rather than one-hot encoded. Its coefficient directly captures the A/H market premium.

**Results:**

|  | Without `market_A` | With `market_A` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Predicted mean | 0.0666 | 0.0806 |
| Mean Gap | +0.93pp | **+2.33pp** |

**Limitation:** Later market-specification diagnostics show that the single `market_A` dummy imposes an overly strong assumption: H-share and A-share firms are forced to share all other coefficients. The transportability test shows that H-to-CSI cross-market prediction has a systematic bias of −3.07pp. Therefore, the pooled estimate of +2.33pp is a product of this strong assumption.

---

### 2.6 Revision 5: Financial-Sector Filtering and Stricter Non-SOE Definition

**Motivation:** The original non-SOE group includes public enterprises, foreign-owned enterprises, and collective enterprises. Their ownership structures and financial characteristics are not fully comparable to private firms.

**Implementation:**

**Financial-sector filtering**, based on second-level industry classification:

```text
Banks, non-bank financials, insurance, securities,
diversified financials, and other financial sectors
```

This removes 123 rows per year, or 861 rows across seven years.

**Stricter non-SOE definition:** Only private enterprises are used as the benchmark.

| Enterprise Type | Classification |
|---|---|
| Central and local SOEs | SOE, prediction target |
| **Private enterprises** | **Non-SOE benchmark** |
| Public, foreign-owned, and collective enterprises | Excluded |
| H-share SOEs | Excluded |

**Result:** 408 firms are retained per year after filtering. The non-SOE benchmark contains around 290 private enterprises per year.

**Limitation:** CSI300 private enterprises account for only around 98 firms per year, which explains why the A-share private benchmark has only 685 training observations.

---

### 2.7 Revision 6: Common-Support Screening

**Motivation:** Some SOEs fall outside the P1–P99 range of non-SOEs in variables such as FirmSize. In those cases, the model extrapolates rather than interpolates.

**Implementation:** For each benchmark, the P1–P99 interval is calculated from the non-SOE training data for eight core variables. An SOE observation is marked as `SupportFlag=1` only if all eight variables fall within the benchmark’s P1–P99 ranges.

The eight core variables are:

`FirmSize_lag`, `FinancialLeverage_lag`, `AssetTurnover_lag`, `FixedAssetsRatio_lag`, `CapexRatio_lag`, `HHI_lag`, `MarketShare_lag`, and `CurrentRatio_lag`.

**Results:**

| Benchmark | Full N | CS N | Support Rate | Main Violating Variables |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| H_nonSOE | 822 | 360 | 43.8% | HHI, FirmSize |
| CSI_nonSOE | 822 | 561 | 68.2% | FirmSize |
| **Pooled** | 822 | **591** | **71.9%** | FirmSize |

**Pooled EBI/A: Full Sample vs Common Support**

| Sample | Mean Gap | Asset-Weighted Gap | Positive Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full | +2.33pp | +3.90pp | 70.2% |
| Common Support | **+1.67pp** | +2.32pp | 65.1% |

**Limitation:** The current common-support test is a marginal-support test. Each variable is checked separately. It is not a multivariate joint-support test. Therefore, an SOE may pass all P1–P99 univariate checks while its overall feature combination does not exist in the benchmark sample.

---

### 2.8 Revision 7: Five Matching and Weighting Methods

**Motivation:** SOEs and private enterprises have very different feature distributions. For example, the SMD of FirmSize is 1.54, meaning that direct comparison is likely biased.

**Methods:**

| Method | Principle | Parameter |
|---|---|---|
| **PSM** | GBM estimates `P(SOE \| X)`, followed by nearest-neighbor matching on logit propensity score | Caliper = 0.5 × SD, with replacement |
| **CEM** | Exact matching on year and industry; continuous variables are binned into five groups | 141 strata |
| **EntropyBal** | Iteratively adjusts non-SOE weights so that weighted non-SOE means equal SOE means | Max 200 iterations |
| **OverlapWgt** | `w = 1 − e(x)` for SOEs and `w = e(x)` for non-SOEs | GBM propensity |

**Matching diagnostics, pooled sample:**

| Method | N Before | N After | ESS | Max SMD Before | Max SMD After |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PSM | 1,824 | 494 | — | 1.823 | **0.562** |
| CEM | 1,824 | 1,383 | — | 1.823 | **1.564** |
| EntropyBal | 1,824 | 1,824 | 1,824 | — | **Failed to converge** |
| OverlapWgt | 1,824 | 1,824 | **36** | — | Only 2% effective |

**Result comparison, pooled EBI/A, 5%–95% winsorization:**

| Method | Mean Gap | Conclusion |
|---|---:|---|
| Baseline | **+2.33pp** | Full sample |
| Common support | **+1.67pp** | After common-support screening |
| CEM | **+1.09pp** | Smaller after exact matching |
| PSM | **−0.71pp** | Reversal, but SMD remains 0.56 |
| Entropy | +2.33pp | Failed to converge; same as baseline |
| Overlap | +2.33pp | Almost no overlap; same as baseline |

**Limitations:**

- Entropy balancing fails to converge because the mean FirmSize difference between SOEs and private firms is 2.6 standard deviations. There is no feasible set of positive weights that can simultaneously match all feature means.
- Overlap weighting has only 2% effective observations. The propensity-score distributions almost do not overlap.
- Even after CEM exactly matches year and industry, within-stratum SMD remains 1.56.
- PSM improves balance most clearly, from 1.82 to 0.56, but it is still far above the standard 0.1 threshold, and the estimated gap reverses to negative.

### 2.9 Model Diagnostic Experiments

#### 2.9.1 Private-Firm Placebo Test

CSI private enterprises are randomly split into two groups. One group is treated as pseudo-SOEs, while the other group serves as the benchmark.

> Across 10 random splits, the average gap is **+0.50pp**, with a standard deviation of **±0.74pp**.
>
> The true SOE gap is **+2.33pp**.

**Conclusion:** The model does not show systematic prediction bias. The SOE gap of +2.33pp is not simply generated by model bias.

#### 2.9.2 Cross-Market Transportability

| Direction | Mean Gap |
|---|---:|
| H→H | −0.21pp ✓ |
| CSI→CSI | −0.45pp ✓ |
| **H→CSI** | **−3.07pp ✗** |
| **CSI→H** | **+12.17pp ✗** |

**Conclusion:** H-share and A-share models are not directly transportable. The H-share model strongly underestimates A-share firms by −3.07pp, while the CSI model strongly overestimates H-share firms by +12.17pp. A single pooled `market_A` dummy is not sufficient to correct this structural difference.

#### 2.9.3 Pooled Market-Specification Diagnosis

| Model Specification | Mean Gap |
|---|---:|
| Pooled + `market_A`, current specification | **+2.33pp** |
| Separate H and CSI modeling, then averaged | **+0.17pp** |
| CSI-only | −0.41pp |
| H-only | +0.75pp |

**Conclusion:** The +2.33pp estimate is generated by the imposed common-slope assumption. After separate market modeling, the gap collapses to only +0.17pp.

#### 2.9.4 Rolling-Window Out-of-Time Validation, CSI-Only

| Training Window | Test Year | OOS Gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019 only | 2020 | +1.96pp |
| 2019–2020 | 2021 | +0.59pp |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 2019–2024 | 2025 | **+0.19pp** |

**Conclusion:** As the training window expands, the gap gradually decreases and becomes close to zero.

- ROE has the lowest R², from 0.03 to 0.15, confirming that it should not be used as the main target.
- `Gap / RMSE ≈ 0.23`: the group-level average gap contains statistical information, but the noise at the individual-firm level is large.

## 4. Winsorization

**The current implementation is clipping, or winsorization, not trimming. Therefore, the sample size is not reduced.**

|  | Clip, Current Implementation | Trim |
|---|---|---|
| Operation | Extreme values are replaced by P5/P95 | Extreme-value rows are deleted |
| Sample size | Unchanged | Reduced by around 10% |
| Key feature | Retains all observations while limiting the influence of extremes | Loses sample observations |

- Features are clipped independently to the P5–P95 range, and all 1,824 training observations are retained.
- The target variable is also clipped to the P5–P95 range, with no deletion.
- In cross-validation, P5/P95 thresholds are recomputed within the training fold of each split, avoiding validation-fold leakage.

## 7. Interim Summary

### 7.1 Core Finding

After seven rounds of model revision and eight sets of diagnostic checks, the most important finding is:

> **SOEs and private enterprises belong to two almost non-overlapping populations in terms of observable firm-level financial characteristics. All matching and weighting methods fail to construct a sufficiently balanced control group.**

This itself is a valuable methodological conclusion.

### 7.2 Summary of Gaps across Methods

| Method | Most Relevant Benchmark | Gap Range |
|---|---|---:|
| Baseline, pooled + `market_A` | Pooled | **+2.33pp** |
| Common support | Pooled | **+1.67pp** |
| CEM | Pooled | **+1.09pp** |
| Separate H and CSI modeling | Pooled | **+0.17pp** |
| CSI-only, A-share private benchmark | CSI_nonSOE | **−0.41pp** |
| Rolling window, six-year window | CSI_nonSOE | **+0.19pp** |
| PSM | Pooled | **−0.71pp** |

> Robust interval: **[−0.7pp, +2.3pp]**.  
> The most credible estimates, based on the same-market A-share private benchmark, fall between **−0.4pp and +0.2pp**.

### 7.3 Methodological Contributions

1. **The pooled model with a single `market_A` dummy is falsified.** The cross-market transportability test shows systematic bias between H-share and CSI markets. After separate market modeling, the gap collapses to +0.17pp.
2. **The boundary of matching and weighting methods is demonstrated.** When the treated group and control group barely overlap in observable characteristics, PSM, CEM, entropy balancing, and overlap weighting all fail to achieve sufficient balance.
3. **A systematic framework for benchmark sensitivity analysis is established.** The analysis combines three benchmarks, five weighting or matching methods, three target variables, and three winsorization levels.

### 7.4 Main Limitations

1. **Year fixed effects and industry-by-year fixed effects have not yet been implemented.** The feature module has been written but has not yet been integrated into the main pipeline.
2. **The CSI private-enterprise sample is small, with only around 98 firms per year.** This limits the precision of the same-market benchmark.
3. **Financing mechanisms have not yet been analyzed.** The relationship between the gap and financing cost or debt structure has not yet been tested, so the asset-return gap cannot be directly linked to financial subsidies.
4. **Common support is currently only marginal support.** Multivariate joint support has not yet been evaluated, for example through Mahalanobis distance or local density.
5. **Dual-listed A+H firms have not yet been handled.** This may create duplicated observations.

### 7.5 Next Steps

1. **Expand the A-share private benchmark** by using all non-financial private firms in the A-share market as the main benchmark.
2. **Integrate year and industry fixed effects** into the main pipeline.
3. **Add financing-cost and debt-structure variables** to test whether the gap is associated with financing advantages.
4. **Conduct event studies around ownership changes,** such as mixed-ownership reform or state-capital takeover, as a causal identification strategy.
5. **Apply Double Machine Learning, or DML,** to estimate ATE within the subsample that satisfies common support.
